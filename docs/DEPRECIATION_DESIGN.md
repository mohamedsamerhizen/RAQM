# Depreciation design

Depreciation runs use the configured fiscal periods rather than calendar-month assumptions. Each depreciable component has a cost, residual and useful life expressed in monthly fiscal-period units. A full period consumes one unit. A first partial period consumes eligible days divided by the fiscal period's calendar days, inclusive from the available-for-use date. The fraction is rounded to six decimal places. Conversion assets start from the later of historic availability and the register cutover, using the supplied remaining life. The method follows the available-for-use principle and defines an explicit fiscal proration convention. [IAS 16](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-16-property-plant-and-equipment/)

The approved depreciable basis is carrying amount less residual, bounded below by zero. Each segment allocates that basis over its approved remaining units. Monetary charges use company minor units and midpoint-to-even rounding. The final occurrence takes the remaining depreciable amount. A charge is capped at carrying amount less the residual. Remaining life can end with the asset still in use.

For USD 24,000, a residual of zero and a 48-period life, a full-period charge is USD 500. In a 30-day fiscal period with availability on day 16, the charge is USD 250. The unused half unit remains for later depreciation. The journal debits depreciation expense and credits accumulated depreciation. A category configured as nondepreciable has a life of zero and produces no depreciation. A parent with components has only the sum of its components' charges.

Prepare one company run for the fiscal period end, preview the component effects and aggregated GL lines, then obtain Owner approval and post. Posted runs consume each component/period combination once. A zero-charge run can retain reviewed evidence and life consumption without an empty journal. Earlier required occurrences and missing fiscal calendar periods block posting. The run and journal share the financial transaction. Retrying a committed operation returns its journal. Closed periods and finalized years reject asset posting.

Estimate revisions, capital improvements, impairment and recovery establish new prospective segments. They take effect on fiscal period starts, after earlier depreciation has been posted. Prior movements stay immutable. A revision can replace remaining life or residual value. Straight-line is the sole supported method. The forecast endpoint projects configured periods from the requested as-of position and current approved terms. It does not predict future estimate changes.

A period already covered by a posted run cannot accept asset changes that would invalidate that run. If an optional category closes with missed depreciation, later posting still requires the earlier run to be resolved. The system has no automatic catch-up into a closed or finalized period.
