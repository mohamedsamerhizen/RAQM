# Disposal and impairment

## Impairment

An assessment identifies a component, an effective fiscal period start, an externally assessed recoverable amount and supporting evidence. The Owner approves the evidence. When recoverable amount is below carrying value, posting debits impairment loss and credits accumulated impairment. The register retains the original cost and the separate impairment balance. For a USD 8,000 carrying value and USD 6,000 recoverable amount, the loss is USD 2,000. Later depreciation uses the revised carrying amount. This phase does not estimate valuations or test cash-generating units. [IAS 36](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-36-impairment-of-assets/)

Recovery needs updated evidence. Its journal debits accumulated impairment and credits the impairment loss account. The recovery is limited by the outstanding reversible loss, recoverable amount and the hypothetical carrying value without recorded impairment. The register derives that ceiling using parallel unimpaired depreciation through the same estimate and improvement history. Opening impairment is retained as a historical balance. Its earlier reversal history is outside the available evidence, so it is excluded from reversible impairment.

## Disposal

Owner-approved sale or write-off derecognizes every component of the asset. The physical date can precede the accounting date within the same fiscal period. Earlier required depreciation must have posted. If a component was available in the current period, the disposal operation includes prorated depreciation through the day before physical disposal. The journal clears cost, accumulated depreciation and accumulated impairment and recognizes the gain or loss based on net proceeds. A write-off has zero proceeds. IAS 16 guides derecognition and the disposal result. [IAS 16](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-16-property-plant-and-equipment/)

For cost USD 10,000, accumulated depreciation USD 7,000 and proceeds USD 4,000, debit cash 4,000 and accumulated depreciation 7,000, credit cost 10,000 and disposal gain 1,000. Use a separate disposal gain account and map it to other income in the Phase 04 definition if appropriate. Supported direct disposal expenses credit registered cash and reduce net proceeds.

If proceeds are already in the books, link the unused credit lines from the posted receipt journal on an ordinary disposal liability clearing account. The disposal debits clearing in place of cash. The original receipt and claim remain traceable. The application supports registered cash or bank and previously posted receipt clearing. Automatic credit sales into the AR subledger and tax-specific disposal rules remain future work.

Sale and write-off are terminal in this version. Idempotent retries return the same operation. Ordinary journal reversal and register reactivation are blocked because they would leave the register, source claims and later periods inconsistent. Suspected errors require accounting review and a coordinated correction workflow before any reinstatement. No automatic reinstatement, partial asset disposal, or replacement-component derecognition is supported.
